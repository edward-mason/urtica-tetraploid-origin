# VALIDATION.md — wet-lab and further computational experiments that would confirm or refute the conclusions

## Claim 1 — the tetraploid is a same-lineage (auto)tetraploid

1. **Flow cytometry and chromosome counts on the sequenced individuals.** Leaf tissue from the DToL specimen's clone/population (Queen Charlotte's Cottage, Kew; voucher K001400687) and from the Bonn plant (IZMB Bonn). Expected: 2C ≈ 2.1–2.6 pg and 2n = 52 (or 48) for both. Refutes if either is diploid (which would invalidate Claim 2 for Bonn and the premise for Kew). Cost: ~£50 per sample for propidium-iodide flow cytometry; chromosome squashes ~1 week of technician time.
2. **Meiotic configuration in tetraploid pollen mother cells.** Acetocarmine or DAPI squashes of anthers from 10 tetraploid plants. Autotetraploids form multivalents (quadrivalents in a substantial fraction of cells) and show tetrasomic segregation; an established allotetraploid forms bivalents only. Expected under Claim 1: ≥ 1 quadrivalent in > 30 % of cells. Cost: low (~£500 consumables), 2–4 weeks.
3. **Tetrasomic inheritance test with codominant markers.** Genotype 10–20 SSR or SNP loci (from the single-copy gene set in gene_distances.csv) in a tetraploid × tetraploid F1 family (n ≈ 100). Autotetraploid expectation: tetrasomic ratios (e.g. 1:35 double-reduction-free duplex × nulliplex gives 1 AAaa : 4 Aaaa : 1 aaaa-type classes); allotetraploid expectation: disomic ratios with fixed heterozygosity. Cost: ~£2–4k (amplicon sequencing); one growing season.
4. **Population-scale test.** Low-coverage (5–10×) whole-genome sequencing of 20 diploid and 20 tetraploid plants spanning Europe; call allelic dosage at single-copy genes; test whether tetraploid allele frequencies and site-frequency spectrum are those of a tetrasomic population drawing from the diploid pool (no fixed heterozygosity; no bimodal allele-divergence). Cost: ~£10–15k; 3 months. This also sharpens Q99(b) with a population rather than one diploid pair.
5. **k-mer analysis of the DToL raw reads — the decisive assembly-independent test (computational; first priority).** Download the HiFi runs ERR10662027–ERR10662029 (~45 Gb), count 21-mers (FastK or KMC3), run GenomeScope 2.0 with -p 4 and Smudgeplot. Expected under Claim 1 (autotetraploid/same lineage): the k-mer pair structure dominated by AAAB and AABB smudges with similar total heterozygosity to a tetrasomic population (nucleotide heterozygosity across the four haplotypes ≈ 0.5–1 %), no distinct low-coverage "AB" pair class at 1–3 % divergence, and GenomeScope2 tetraploid model fit with aaab ≳ aabb. Expected under an undeposited divergent subgenome: a strong AABB smudge with the paired-k-mer distance peaking at the subgenome divergence, GenomeScope2 aabb ≫ aaab, and total k-mer heterozygosity well above the within-lineage value. Also compute Merqury.FK completeness of DP + DA against the read k-mer database and tabulate the missing k-mers by copy number: a missing *divergent single-copy* class (not just collapsed repeats) is what the hidden-subgenome hypothesis requires. Cost: compute only (~60 GB disk, ~10 CPU-h); not done here because the download plus k-mer counting exceeded the study's disk/time ceiling on the Windows workstation. The same analysis on the Bonn nanopore reads (PRJEB63450) tests Claim 2 (ONT R10 reads are accurate enough for 21-mer spectra at ~50× depth).

## Claim 3 — centromere classes and the diversity landscape

7. **CENH3 ChIP-seq or CUT&Tag** on nettle (antibody raised against the nettle CENH3 N-terminus, identified from the RefSeq annotation by homology to Arabidopsis HTR12). Expected if "polycentric" chromosomes are truly polycentric: several discrete CENH3 domains per chromosome within the predicted spans; if metapolycentric/monocentric with large pericentromeres: one extended or one compact domain. Cost: antibody ~£3k, sequencing ~£2k; 3–4 months.
8. **Immuno-FISH on mitotic metaphases**: CENH3 immunostaining plus FISH with the dominant tandem repeats in the RepeatOBserver intervals (to be extracted from the EDTA/Table S8 regions). Directly visualises the number of primary constrictions/kinetochore sites per chromosome. Cost: ~£2k; 2 months.
9. **Crossover mapping.** Single-pollen-nucleus sequencing (as in Castellani et al. 2024) or an F2 linkage map (n ≈ 200) from the existing 11-4 × 7-5 pedigree would give the recombination landscape directly and test whether crossovers are excluded from the 10–25 Mb low-diversity cores. Cost: ~£15–25k.
