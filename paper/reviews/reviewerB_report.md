**Single most serious threat.** The H1 test is run on a HiCanu + purge_dups pseudo-primary (DP) plus a partial alternate (DA) that together contain only 68 % of the reliable read k-mers of the tetraploid (DToL note, Table 2: primary 55.00 %, alternate 49.39 %, combined 68.00 %), whereas the two haplotypes of the diploid female capture 98.15 % (Hirabayashi 2025). Claim 1 as worded ("autotetraploid, not allotetraploid with a divergent second parental lineage") is therefore not established; what the data do support is the narrower statement that **no subgenome diverged by ≳ 0.4 % at ≥ 50-gene block scale is present in the deposited pseudo-haplotype DP**, and that at gene level DP is 1.10× more divergent from the Romanian diploid than the Bulgarian diploid haplotype is.

## A. Prior publication (task item 1)

**1. [major] Rejlová et al. 2021 already reported the molecular result that Claim 1 confirms.** Abstract (doi 10.1093/botlinnean/boaa076): Hyb-Seq of 279 plants of *U. dioica* s.l.; "our molecular evaluation did not reveal any structure. Moreover, tetraploids coalesced with diploids in both morphological and molecular analyses." The authors list as explanations "(2) only recent genetic diversification of the group and (3) homoploid and heteroploid hybridization events." This is not a subgenome-divergence test and does not use any genome assembly, but it is a published, multi-locus, population-scale finding that tetraploids are not genetically distinct from diploids. Claim 1 must be framed as a genome-wide, assembly-based quantification of an existing result, not as a discovery; the paper must cite it (and use 2021 as the year of record). The full text could not be read here; the authors must check whether the Hyb-Seq paper or the Rejlová PhD thesis ("Evolutionary Processes Shaping the Genus *Urtica* L. (Urticaceae) in Europe and Adjacent Areas", Charles University; existence confirmed from the dspace.cuni.cz reviewer report, content not accessible) contains an explicit auto/allo analysis (e.g. allele-ratio or phased-allele phylogenies).

**2. [minor] The allopolyploid hypothesis has a named source and the autopolyploid hypothesis has been raised before.** Rejlová et al. 2019 (PLoS One, full text read): "The widespread tetraploid cytotype of *U. dioica* is also often supposed to be an allopolyploid or a group of allopolyploids with different evolutionary histories (e.g. [117])" where [117] = Geltman D., Bot. Zhurn. 75:840–845 (1990); and, about tetraploids resembling subsp. *subinermis*, "which raises the question as to whether (auto)polyploidization has taken place in this subspecies". The same paper reports "a uniform Cx-value of core *U. dioica*", which is itself weak evidence against an allotetraploid with a differently sized progenitor. Cronk et al. 2016 (PMC5136675) raise the possibility that the widespread tetraploid "may have" arisen from the SE-European diploids (sentence truncated in the search result; must be quoted from the paper). None of these tests the question genomically. Neither Rejlová paper states or tests subgenome divergence.

**3. The peer-review report attached to the note says: "only one subgenome is presented, which limits the utility for the study of polyploidy ... **4. [minor] Code uses hifiasm vocabulary for a HiCanu assembly.** CLAIM.md and the preregistration speak of "hifiasm/Hi-C curation"; the DToL note says HiCanu + purge_dups + YaHS. This matters for the artefact discussion (point 6): purge_dups is a documented over-purger of homoeologs in polyploids, and HiCanu primaries are contig-level haplotype mosaics with collapsed near-identical regions. The manuscript must describe the DToL pipeline correctly.

## B. Alternative explanations for Claim 1 (task item 2)

Reproduction: from gene_distances.csv with the h1_test.py filters the reviewer obtains n = 13,438 genes (claim: 13,439), median b = 0.449 %, median t = 0.497 %, R = 1.106, Q99(b) = 2.69 %, f99 = 1.667 % — consistent with h1_result_DP.json (R = 1.100). Single-haplotype medians (no min): d(PAT,FH1) 0.583 %, d(PAT,FH2) 0.568 %, W = d(FH1,FH2) 0.468 %; d(DP,FH1) 0.612 %, d(DP,FH2) 0.625 %, d(DP,PAT) 0.625 %; d(DA,FH1/FH2) 0.625/0.627 %; d(DP,DA) 0.332 %; d(FH1,UU) 9.30 %.

**5. (a) Collapsed/consensus pseudo-haplotype — [minor, survives].** QV 60.6 means DP's k-mers are read k-mers, so DP is not a base-level consensus of divergent haplotypes; HiCanu does not collapse haplotypes at ≥ 1 % divergence. Collapse of near-identical (≤ 0.3 %) haplotypes cannot hide a divergent subgenome. Survives.

**6. (b) Curation/purging against a diploid-like structure — [major, not resolved].** With HiCanu + purge_dups, a homoeologous subgenome at 1–3 % divergence would be assembled as separate contigs and then either (i) retained in the primary as second loci, (ii) purged into the alternate, or (iii) purged into categories not deposited. Evidence against (i): the excess of second loci in DP is confined to ≥ 97 % identity (point 7). Evidence against (ii): d(DP,DA) has only 2.9 % of genes above Q99(b) (h1_result_DP.json) — but see point 13: the preregistered dip test never ran, and DA itself is only 49 % k-mer complete. Nothing in the tables addresses (iii): the deposited DP + DA contain 68 % of the reliable read k-mers. The reviewer's expectation under a neutral autotetraploid with π = 0.5 % (k = 31, infinite sites, n = 4 coalescent) is ≈ 75 % completeness for one haplotype and ≈ 88 % for two (calibration: the same model gives 86 % for one diploid haplotype vs 81.7 % observed, 100 % for both vs 98.15 % observed). The observed 55 % / 68 % are 15–20 points below the autotetraploid expectation and match an undeposited haplotype pair diverged by ~1–1.5 % from the deposited pair (expected two-haplotype completeness 75 % at 1 %, 64 % at 1.5 %). This back-of-envelope calculation ignores collapsed repeats and the lower per-haplotype coverage of the tetraploid, so it is not proof of a hidden subgenome; it shows that the k-mer evidence available to the authors points away from, not toward, their conclusion, and must be addressed with the DToL k-mer database (FastK/Merqury.FK) or GenomeScope2/Smudgeplot on the reads.

**7. (c) Best-locus selection blind to a second subgenome present as a second locus — [major in principle; the specific 90–97 % version survives].** From loci_DP.csv vs loci_FH1/FH2.csv (23,016 / 23,111 / 23,112 queries): fraction of genes with a second locus at 90–97 % identity and cov2 ≥ 0.8 is 10.3 % (DP) vs 9.5 % (FH1) and 9.8 % (FH2); with cov2 ≥ 0.5: 11.9 % vs 11.1 % / 11.4 %. No excess → no homoeologous subgenome at 3–10 % divergence is present in DP as a second locus. The excess is entirely at ≥ 97 %: 22.3 % of DP genes vs 10.2 % (FH1) / 9.6 % (FH2). 2,905 genes (12.6 %) have a ≥ 97 %, ≥ 80 %-coverage second locus in DP but in none of the four diploid haplotypes; their BLAST pident2 is unimodal (median 98.93; 31.5 % in [99.0,99.5), 25.4 % in [98.5,99.0), 15.1 % in [98.0,98.5), 8.8 % in [97.5,98.0), 4.8 % in [97.0,97.5)), i.e. the second copy is on average 0.37 identity points further from the MH2 query than the best copy (100−pident: 1.07 vs 0.70). These duplicates are spatially clustered (100-gene windows reach 52 % duplicated vs a permutation-null maximum of 33 %; chr8 18.3 % of genes), as expected for unpurged haplotigs. A homoeolog at δ ≈ 1 % would sit at pident2 ≈ 98.3 and is not excluded for a minority of these genes, but a genome-wide homoeologous copy class is not visible. The single-copy filter removed 3,027 genes solely because DP has a second locus with score ratio ≥ 0.8 (pident2 quantiles 10/50/90 = 96.2/98.8/99.6); at their best locus these genes have R = 1.107 and f99 = 5.2 % (vs 1.7 %), so the filter does not hide a class with elevated best-locus divergence. Survives for DP; see point 6 for copies that are not in DP at all.

**8. (d) Reference bias from MH2 queries — [minor, survives].** The two-target technical floor d(MAT, FH_source) is exactly 0 for 99.8 % of genes (mean 1e-5), so alignment noise does not inflate two-target distances (t) relative to query-anchored ones (b on the ten chromosomes where PAT = MH2 is the query itself). R on chromosomes 3, 5, 6 (PAT = MH1, symmetric design) is 1.052 (chromosome-block bootstrap CI 1.003–1.118, n = 2,568) vs 1.120 on the other ten; the difference is within sampling error but should be reported. Alignment dropout at divergent segments would lower t, which is conservative against the authors' own claim; the 9.3 % U. urens control shows the assay reaches that range. Survives.

**9. (e) min over two female haplotypes — [minor, survives with a caveat].** t and b use the same min; symmetric. Caveat: d(DP,DA) is a min-of-three by construction (the purged alternate is the sequence most similar to the primary), so "d(DP,DA) = 0.31 % < W = 0.47 %" is not evidence of low heterozygosity in the tetraploid and the d(DP,DA) bimodality control is systematically blind to the most divergent haplotype at each locus. In addition, DA contains 220 genes with tA > 5 % whose d(DA,UU) median is 23 % and d(DP,DA) median 10.8 % — paralogs picked as "best locus" because the ortholog is absent from the incomplete DA; these inflate the held-out f99 (1.89 %) and must be removed by a synteny or d(DP,DA) filter before DA is called a replicate.

**(f) gene conversion / homogenisation and (g) introgression — [minor, cannot be addressed by these data].** Homoeologous gene conversion affects a minority of loci and cannot erase a 1–2 % genome-wide signal; it is irrelevant unless the subgenomes were already very close, in which case the test has no power anyway (point 10). Diploid→tetraploid introgression via triploids is documented (Rejlová 2019: 18 % triploid achenes from diploid mothers in a mixed population) and could homogenise subgenome A with sympatric diploids, but UK tetraploids have no sympatric diploids and introgression cannot remove subgenome B. Neither mechanism rescues or breaks the claim.

**Blocks where t and b disagree (reviewer scan; [major — must be reported]).** Rolling 50-gene median of the paired difference t − b exceeds the permutation-null maximum (99th pct 0.37 pp) in two regions: chr8 8.6–13.7 Mb (max +0.94 pp) and chr7 1.8–21.8 Mb (max +0.61 pp). In the chr8 block b is deflated, not t inflated: median b 0.10 % (d(PAT,FH2) 0.11 %) while W 0.78 % and t 0.63 % — the Bulgarian "paternal" haplotype is nearly identical to FH2 over ~5 Mb of the inversion-rich chr8 region (IBD or a local phasing/assembly artefact; Hirabayashi 2026 Table 2 reports MH2 aligning to FH2 at > 99.7 % over only 0.3 % of chr8, so the CDS-level identity found here needs explanation). In the chr7 block all three diploid haplotypes and DA share one low-diversity haplotype (b 0.10 %, W 0.11 %, tA 0.17 %) while DP carries a 0.60 %-divergent one (d(DP,DA) 0.55 %): the tetraploid retains variation absent from the diploid sample over ~20 Mb. This is compatible with an autotetraploid of larger effective size, with ancestral polymorphism predating a diploid sweep/bottleneck, or with a segment of a closely related second lineage; the authors must show windowed t, b, W and d(DP,DA) tracks for all chromosomes and discuss these blocks rather than rely on Moran's I of the > Q99 indicator, which is blind to them. Windowed median t never exceeds 1.25 %, and every window with t > 0.8 % except the chr8 block also has b 0.7–0.95 % and W 0.8–1.0 %: there is no block of ≥ 50 genes where DP is elevated while the diploid comparisons are normal. This is the strongest positive evidence in the data and should replace the Moran's I statistic.


**9b. [major] The held-out replicate does show spatial clustering, and the claim omits it.** CLAIM.md says "the high-divergence genes are not spatially clustered (Moran's I lag-1 p = 0.29)" and reports DA only as "R = 1.093 (CI 1.064–1.127), f99 = 1.89 %". h1_result_DA.json gives moran_I = 0.1936 above the 99th percentile of the null (0.1935), moran_p = 0.006 — significant clustering of high-t genes in the alternate haplotype. This does not flip the preregistered verdict (f99 ≪ 0.20) but it is selective reporting, and together with the DA paralog-hit tail (point 9) it means the "confirmed by the held-out dataset" statement needs the DA result reported in full and re-run after paralog filtering.

## C. Statistical critique (task item 3)

**10. [major] The preregistered thresholds are not derived from any model and have no power against a realistic allotetraploid.** Reviewer simulation (b resampled as the same-lineage baseline; a fraction φ of DP genes receives extra divergence δ): R / f99 =

| δ (extra p-dist) | φ = 0.10 | φ = 0.25 | φ = 0.50 | φ = 0.75 | φ = 1.00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.25 % | 1.06 / 0.010 | 1.15 / 0.011 | 1.30 / 0.012 | 1.43 / 0.013 | 1.56 / 0.013 |
| 0.5 % | 1.11 / 0.011 | 1.32 / 0.012 | 1.62 / 0.014 | 1.89 / 0.015 | 2.11 / 0.017 |
| 1.0 % | 1.14 / 0.013 | 1.46 / 0.016 | 2.38 / 0.023 | 2.90 / 0.029 | 3.23 / 0.035 |
| 1.5 % | 1.14 / 0.019 | 1.46 / 0.033 | 3.34 / 0.056 | 3.97 / 0.079 | 4.34 / 0.102 |
| 2.0 % | 1.14 / 0.040 | 1.46 / 0.084 | 4.45 / 0.160 | 5.07 / 0.233 | 5.45 / 0.308 |
| 3.0 % | 1.14 / 0.109 | 1.46 / 0.257 | 6.52 / 0.505 | 7.29 / 0.751 | 7.68 / 1.000 |

Every cell with φ ≤ 0.25 and δ ≤ 1.5 %, and φ ≤ 0.10 and δ ≤ 2 %, satisfies the "same-lineage" rule. The "allotetraploid, one subgenome shared" rule (f99 ≥ 0.20) needs δ ≥ 2 % with φ ≥ 0.75 or δ ≥ 3 % with φ ≥ 0.25. Because R is a ratio of medians it saturates at φ < 0.5 (a minority subgenome never moves the median), and Q99(b) = 2.7 % is 6× the median, so only genes with δ ≳ 2 % count toward f99. Time scale: with d(FH1,UU) = 9.3 % and an assumed *U. dioica*–*U. urens* split of 10 Myr (no published estimate found; Hirabayashi 2025 cites Huang et al. 2019 only for family-level ages, 57–85 Myr), 1 Myr of separate history corresponds to δ ≈ 0.93 % and the within-lineage b (0.45 %) to ≈ 0.5 Myr; at a 20-Myr split the numbers halve. An allotetraploid whose subgenomes separated 1–2 Myr ago (δ ≈ 0.5–2 %) would thus produce R ≈ 1.1–1.5 and f99 ≈ 0.01–0.04 unless the divergent subgenome dominates DP — squarely inside the "same-lineage" box. The thresholds must be replaced by an explicit model: expected (R, f99) for auto vs allo as a function of δ and the assembly's subgenome composition φ, with φ estimated from the data (e.g. from windowed t − b blocks, point 9) and δ bounded. The authors should report the largest δ excluded at a given φ rather than a binary verdict.

**11. [minor] Q99(b) and sample composition.** t and b are computed on the same genes, so composition is matched. But the high-t genes are not a random subset: the 224 f99 genes have median b = 1.09 % (2.4× the overall median) and 42 % of them have b > Q95(b). High t therefore tracks gene-specific properties (fast evolution, paralog confusion: the 86 DP genes with t > 5 % have d(DP,UU) 18 % vs 9.3 % typical), not position. A paired statistic (t − b, or the per-gene ratio) is more appropriate than two marginal thresholds.

**12. [minor] Block length.** Hirabayashi 2026 report ~one crossover per chromosome per meiosis (none on six chromosomes), the diploid haplotypes share identical 20-Mb blocks (chr7, point 9) and the 2,905 DP-specific duplicates cluster over tens of Mb; 2-Mb blocks therefore overstate independence. Reviewer CIs: chromosome-level bootstrap R 1.064–1.159, 10-Mb blocks 1.071–1.159, vs the authors' 1.07–1.14. f99 CI by chromosome bootstrap 0.0139–0.0208. The verdict does not change, but the reported CI should be the chromosome-level one.

**13. [major] The preregistered bimodality criterion was never evaluated.** Both h1_result_DP.json and h1_result_DA.json contain "dip_err": "No module named 'diptest'". The verdict code treats a missing dip p-value as "no second mode", so the third conjunct of the same-lineage rule passed by default. Hartigan's dip test must be run (or the preregistration amended and the deviation declared). Given point 9, the bimodality control is in any case weak.

## D. Claim 2 — Bonn assembly ploidy (task item 4)

**14. [major] The 6.6–7.7 % baseline is not comparable.** The four "diploid" baselines are single, haplotype-resolved haplotypes (one expected copy per gene); the Bonn assembly is an unphased 1.1-Gb nanopore assembly (two expected copies). From copy_structure.json the reviewer confirms BN ≥ 3 loci = 15.76 % + 8.15 % = 23.9 % and BN exactly-2 loci = 51.0 % (FH1: 3.4 %). The fair baseline is an unphased diploid. Concatenating FH1 + FH2 per gene from loci_FH1/FH2.csv and counting at most two ≥ 97 %/≥ 80 %-coverage loci per haplotype gives a *lower bound* of 10.7 % of genes with ≥ 3 loci (88.7 % with ≥ 2). The true unphased-diploid value is higher (third loci per haplotype are not counted in loci_*.csv). The contrast is therefore ≤ 2.2×, not 3–3.6×, and must be recomputed on concatenated FH1+FH2 and MH1+MH2 with identical thresholds.

**15. [major] ≥ 3 near-identical loci in a diploid nanopore assembly have mundane explanations that the tables cannot exclude.** (a) Uncollapsed haplotig redundancy: in a nanopore assembly of a genome with 0.5 % heterozygosity and extensive SVs, regions are routinely assembled as two haplotypes plus a third, partially collapsed or overlapping contig; BN second-locus pident2 has median 99.11 (vs 98.88 in DP), consistent with same-individual haplotypes. (b) Segmental duplications: present at ~6–8 % in every assembly here. (c) Contamination/pooling: if leaf tissue from more than one plant was pooled for HMW DNA (common for nanopore), extra haplotypes at ≥ 99 % identity appear in a diploid; the Methods of Wolff et al. must be checked (not accessible to this reviewer). Distinguishing evidence the authors must produce: read-depth of the Bonn nanopore reads over 1-, 2-, 3- and 4-copy loci (haplotigs show ~0.5× depth each; collapsed loci 2×); whether the third loci sit on short contigs or contig ends; GenomeScope2/Smudgeplot of the Bonn reads (the decisive ploidy test, independent of assembly); pairwise identity among the three copies (haplotigs of one individual: ≈ 99.5 %, i.e. W; the claim's own prediction (b) in Amendment 1 cannot be told from a diploid with haplotig triplication without depth). Amendment 1's inference from "MGSE ≈ 1.3 Gb and 25×/50× coverage peaks" is also not decisive: an unphased diploid assembly of 1.1 Gb with heterozygous k-mers at 25× and homozygous at 50× is exactly the diploid expectation; a tetraploid at the same total depth would show peaks at 12.5×/25×/37.5×/50×. The reviewer recomputes R_BN = 0.973 on 23,125 BN genes (claim 0.94; difference due to gene set) — this only shows the best BN copy is of the same lineage, as for every assembly here.

## E. Claims 3 and 4 (task item 5)

**16. [major] Claim 3 misreports its own direction and omits the preregistered confound controls.** The claim: trough ratio 0.38 on polycentric vs 0.67 on acrocentric chromosomes, exact permutation p = 1.0 for T(acro) < T(poly), summarised as "does not distinguish". A one-sided p of 1.0 over C(12,5) = 792 labelings means the observed difference is the most extreme in the opposite direction, i.e. p ≈ 1/792 ≈ 0.001 for T(poly) < T(acro). By the preregistered falsifier ("polycentric chromosomes show the same mean T, p > 0.2") H3 is falsified, and there is an unreported, significant opposite effect. The preregistration also fixed two controls that are absent from the claim: the distance-to-chromosome-end analysis on polycentric chromosomes, and recomputation with 2 Mb around each predicted centromere removed (gene-density circularity). With ~one terminal crossover per chromosome (Hirabayashi 2026) the pericentral region of every chromosome is the low-recombination region regardless of centromere type, so a deeper central trough on the longer "polycentric" chromosomes is the null expectation of linked selection, not a centromere signal; RepeatOBserver's "diffuse region" midpoint is, by construction, the repeat-dense, gene-poor centre. The result should be re-stated as "π is depressed in chromosome centres on all chromosomes, more so on the polycentric set (p ≈ 0.001), consistent with a recombination-landscape effect", with the two preregistered controls reported. (No H3 tables were supplied; the reviewer could not recompute these numbers.)

**17. [minor–major] Claim 4: the fission/fusion dichotomy is false and two preregistered outgroups are missing.** The preregistration names ramie and *Morus notabilis* as outgroups; the claim reports only *Parietaria judaica* and *Cannabis sativa*. Dropping ramie — the one preregistered outgroup required ("≥ 1 of Parietaria/ramie") besides Parietaria — is an undeclared deviation. If *Parietaria* carries a lineage-specific genome duplication (asserted in the task; not verified by this reviewer), 1:1 RBH orthologs are split across two homoeologous chromosomes and the ≥ 70 % single-chromosome rule cannot be met, so "inconclusive" is pre-determined by outgroup choice rather than by biology; syntenic-block orthology (e.g. GENESPACE/MCScanX with ancestral-karyotype reconstruction) is required. *Cannabis* (Cannabaceae; Urticaceae–Moraceae split 84.87 Myr per Hirabayashi 2025) is too distant for 20-gene collinear windows. Finally, the claim's own finding that *U. urens* chr2 is an eight-segment mosaic of chr8 and chr13 segments means ≥ 7 breakpoints separate the karyotypes; a single fission-vs-fusion polarisation is not a meaningful question for such a history. Hirabayashi 2025 already reported the chr8/chr13 ↔ chr2 synteny (quoted in point 3); the novel element is the segment count.
