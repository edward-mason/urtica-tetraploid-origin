# CLAIMS (final, post-review)  —  *Urtica dioica* tetraploid origin

**Claim 1 (primary).** Within the sequence deposited for the Darwin Tree of Life tetraploid *Urtica dioica* (drUrtDioi1.1; GCA_964188135.1 primary and GCA_964188125.1 alternate pseudo-haplotypes; male, RBG Kew, BioSample SAMEA7521824), there is no second subgenome more divergent from the Eurasian diploid gene pool than present-day diploid alleles are from each other. For 13,432 single-copy protein-coding genes the tetraploid primary pseudo-haplotype's divergence from the nearest Romanian diploid haplotype (t, median 0.50 %) is 1.106× (chromosome-level bootstrap 95 % CI 1.061–1.163) that of the unrelated Bulgarian diploid haplotype (b, median 0.45 %); 1.62 % of genes (CI 1.36 %–2.06 %) exceed the 99th percentile of b. Held-out alternate: R = 1.097 (CI 1.065–1.137), f99 = 1.79 %. The genes above Q99(b) are spatially clustered, but in every block the diploid baseline b is deflated (shared Romanian–Bulgarian low-diversity haplotype) and t is at the genome-wide diploid level; the maximum 50-gene median t is 1.25 %. Primary-vs-alternate divergence (median 0.33 %) is unimodal on the log scale; retained second copies are at allelic (≥ 97 %) identity with no excess in the 80–97 % classes. **Excluded** (expected R or f99 above the observed upper CI): a second subgenome with extra divergence δ ≥ 0.5 % contributing ≥ 25 % of deposited genes; δ ≥ 2 % contributing ≥ 10 %; δ = 0.25 % contributing ≥ 50 %. **Not excluded:** δ ≤ 1.5 % at ≤ 10 %; δ = 0.25 % at ≤ 25 %; and any subgenome absent from the deposited sequence (the two pseudo-haplotypes hold 68 % of the tetraploid's read k-mers). Interpretation: the deposited tetraploid is an autotetraploid of the Eurasian diploid gene pool or a tetraploid formed between diploid populations no more differentiated than present-day Romanian and Bulgarian nettles (~0.5 %). This is the first genome-wide, assembly-based test of the question; Rejlová et al. 2021 (Hyb-Seq) had already found tetraploids and diploids genetically inseparable.

**Claim 2 (secondary, exploratory; questions the depositors' reading).** The 1.1-Gb Bonn nanopore assembly (Wolff et al. 2026, bonndata 10.60507/FK2/1XHSZ3), described by its authors as two haplophases of a diploid, carries ≥ 3 near-identical (≥ 97 %) loci for 23.9 % of RefSeq genes, on contigs of median 40 Mb, against 11.5 %–13.3 % for the diploid assemblies with both haplotypes pooled and 32.1 % for the two DToL tetraploid pseudo-haplotypes pooled; its genes are at diploid-level divergence (R = 1.11, single-copy genes). The copy structure is intermediate between a pooled diploid and a pooled tetraploid; the depositors' diagnostics (80.8 % duplicated BUSCOs, two-copy peak, 25×/50× depth peaks, MGSE 1.3 Gb) fit a haplotype-resolved diploid and a two-copy-collapsed tetraploid equally. The plant's ploidy is therefore *unresolved*; tetraploidy is a possible, not an established, explanation, and the depositors should check it by flow cytometry or a k-mer spectrum.

**Claim 3 (negative result, preregistered H3).** Nucleotide diversity in coding sequences is depressed around the predicted centromeric region of every *U. dioica* chromosome, and significantly more deeply on the seven chromosomes predicted to be polycentric (trough ratio T 0.38 vs 0.67; exact permutation p = 0.001 for the opposite of the preregistered direction; male replicate T 0.44 vs 0.77, p = 0.018); the effect survives both preregistered controls. The diversity landscape does not show distributed recombination on the polycentric chromosomes; it is consistent with a recombination landscape of distal crossovers on all chromosomes.

**Claim 4 (inconclusive, preregistered H2).** *U. urens* chromosome 2 is an eight-segment mosaic of *U. dioica* chr8 and chr13 segments (one inverted, no interstitial telomere arrays); with *Parietaria judaica* (apparently polyploid) and *Cannabis sativa* as outgroups, the preregistered ≥ 70 % rule is not met in either direction. Ramie and *Morus* were dropped (no annotated chromosome-level assembly). No polarisation is claimed; parsimony leans toward fusion plus inversions in the *U. urens* lineage.
