# Biological constraints for downstream analyses of the Urtica dioica genome

Query date: 2026-10-22. Each rule below is followed by the sentence(s) of published evidence that motivate it and the verified DOI. Rules are written for the analyst building the comparative pipeline; "MUST" marks a step whose omission would invalidate a result, "SHOULD" marks a step whose omission must be declared as a limitation.

## C1. Cytotype trap

**Rule C1.1 — MUST record the ploidy of every *U. dioica* sample, assembly and read set before any comparison, and MUST NOT pool the DToL assembly with the UBC diploid assemblies as if they were the same genome.**

Evidence. "U. dioica is either diploid (2n = 24, 26) or tetraploid (2n = 48, 52), with tetraploids being much more abundant" ([Hirabayashi 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/plants14010124)). "The widespread tetraploid cytotype (87%) was strongly prevalent over diploids (13%)"; chromosome counts "2n = 26 was ascertained for diploids … 2n = 52 was ascertained for tetraploids" ([Rejlová 2019](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0218389)). The DToL assembly GCA_964188135.1 is "scaffolded into 13 chromosomal pseudomolecules, representing one subgenome of the tetraploid genome", was "curated in multiple rounds, initially on all four haplotypes and subsequently on a pseudo-primary haplotype 1", and its reviewer notes "k-mer completeness is relatively low (68% combined haplotypes), and BUSCO shows high duplication (15%), likely due to tetraploidy" ([Christenhusz 2025](https://doi.org/10.12688/wellcomeopenres.23399.1)). The UBC female (clone 11-4) and male (U48) assemblies are from "a diploid female individual" and "a diploid male individual" ([Hirabayashi 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/plants14010124); [Hirabayashi 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-026-06960-7)).

Consequences for the pipeline. (a) Gene-family counts, BUSCO duplication, k-mer spectra and heterozygosity from the DToL assembly are confounded by collapsed/split homoeologs; use the DToL assembly only for synteny and as a second tetraploid-derived haplotype, never for copy-number claims. (b) Short-read SRA runs of unknown ploidy (98 RNA-seq runs listed; most without cytotype metadata) MUST be ploidy-inferred (k-mer or allele-balance) or excluded from allele-level analyses. (c) The Bonn nanopore assembly of 1.1 Gb ([Wolff 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.15.725508)) has no stated ploidy; treat its span as uninterpretable until the authors state the cytotype.

**Rule C1.2 — MUST use the monoploid (Cx) genome size, not the 2C value, when comparing genome size to assembly span, and SHOULD cite both laboratories' values.**

Evidence. Diploids "varied from 1.31 to 1.35 pg per 2C nucleus, equivalent to a haploid genome size of c. 650 Mbp"; tetraploids "from 2.36 to 2.59 pg" ([Cronk 2016](https://doi.org/10.3897/bdj.4.e10003)). Mean Cx 0.55–0.59 pg and 2C 1.10–1.21 pg (diploid) vs 2.08–2.20 pg (tetraploid) ([Rejlová 2019](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0218389)). "Genome size estimates for diploid U. dioica also vary according to different studies, ranging from 558 Mbp to 660 Mbp" ([Hirabayashi 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/plants14010124)). Assembly spans are 575/521 Mb (female H1/H2), 557/534 Mb (male MH1/MH2) and 618 Mb (DToL "one subgenome").

**Rule C1.3 — MUST NOT assume a 1:1 chromosome correspondence between diploid and tetraploid karyotypes; n = 12 and n = 13 both occur.**

Evidence. "U. dioica is almost always n = 13 in diploid cytotypes, while n = 12 is more frequently observed in tetraploids" ([Hirabayashi 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/plants14010124)). Tetraploid outliers at "2C pg: 1.66–1.74 … might correspond to a loss of four chromosomes, i.e. to the frequently reported chromosome number 2n = 48" ([Rejlová 2019](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0218389)). "The repeated reports of both numbers leads to a suspicion that both numbers do exist in nature" ([Cronk 2016](https://doi.org/10.3897/bdj.4.e10003)). The DToL tetraploid-derived assembly nevertheless has 13 pseudomolecules; whether this reflects the sampled plant's karyotype or scaffolding against the diploid structure is not stated.

**Rule C1.4 — MUST NOT treat *U. gracilis* (North American, monoecious) or N. American "subsp. gracilis" sequence as *U. dioica*; MUST check taxon labels on any public read set.**

Evidence. "U. dioica is distinct from slender nettle, Urtica gracilis Aiton (also known as U. dioica ssp. gracilis (Aiton) Selander), which is native to North America. While U. dioica is generally dioecious … U. gracilis is monoecious" ([Hirabayashi 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/plants14010124)); rehabilitation of *U. gracilis* in [Henning 2014](https://doi.org/10.11646/phytotaxa.162.2.1). Diploid Eurasian subspecies (*subinermis*, *sondenii*, *pubescens*, *kurdistanica*) show no molecular structure in Hyb-Seq and "tetraploids coalesced with diploids" ([Rejlová 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/botlinnean/boaa076)), so subspecies labels on public samples carry morphological, not genetic, information.

## C2. Chromosome 8 inversion and structural heterozygosity

**Rule C2.1 — MUST treat chromosome 8 as structurally heterozygous in every *U. dioica* individual sequenced so far, and MUST report which haplotype (orientation) any chromosome-8 coordinate refers to.**

Evidence. "The two haplotypes differed by a massive inversion (18.4 Mbp in length) on chromosome 8, which was surrounded by multiple duplicated regions" ([Hirabayashi 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/plants14010124)). "Chromosome 8 stands out for its multiple large, nested inversions and high levels of repetitive sequences"; the region shows "multiple multi-Mbp chromosomal inversions and other rearrangements, including a possible 8 Mbp insertion in the paternally-inherited haplotype that is not observed in the maternal haplotypes" ([Hirabayashi 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-026-06960-7)).

Consequences. (a) Any window-based statistic (recombination proxy, TE density, GC, gene density, dN/dS along the chromosome) computed on chromosome 8 MUST be computed on both haplotype orientations, or on the collinear flanks only, and the inversion interval MUST be masked or analysed separately. (b) Read mapping of other individuals to a single haplotype will produce orientation-dependent coverage and split-read artefacts across the 18.4 Mb interval and its flanking duplications; MUST map to both haplotypes or to a pangenome graph. (c) Synteny between *U. dioica* and *U. urens*/ramie on chromosome 8 is orientation-dependent; dot plots MUST be shown per haplotype.

**Rule C2.2 — MUST NOT interpret the chromosome 8 inversion as sex-linked or as adaptive without new evidence; both are published hypotheses, not results.**

Evidence. "Chromosome 8 … features that are often associated with sex determining regions (SDRs). This chromosome is therefore a candidate for further investigations" and "will be a promising candidate for further studies on sex determination" ([Hirabayashi 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-026-06960-7)). "In future studies, it will be interesting to determine whether the inversions we identified play a role in ecotypic adaptation in nettle" ([Hirabayashi 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/plants14010124)). The same chromosome carries a fusion/fission history (C4.1), which is an alternative explanation for repeat accumulation and rearrangement.

**Rule C2.3 — SHOULD expect low collinearity genome-wide, not only on chromosome 8, and MUST NOT use "fraction syntenic between haplotypes" as a quality metric.**

Evidence. "SyRI classified only about 71% of the regions between the two haplotypes as syntenic, with around 16% of the genome remaining unaligned"; structural variants concentrated on "chromosomes 1, 2, 3, and 8" (female) and "1, 2, 3, 6, and 8" (male); heterozygosity ~1.5 % (short reads, female) and 1.85 % (k-mer, male) ([Hirabayashi 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/plants14010124); [Hirabayashi 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-026-06960-7)). Validation of the SVs: "We manually checked all those SVs using Hi-C data and looked for HiFi and Nanopore reads spanning their breakpoints … In all cases, these checks supported the current chromosome organization of the two haplotypes" ([Hirabayashi 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/plants14010124)).

## C3. Heterogeneous centromeres

**Rule C3.1 — MUST label centromere type as "predicted (RepeatOBserver)" in every table and figure, and MUST NOT describe nettle chromosomes 1–7 and 10 as polycentric without that qualifier.**

Evidence. "Repeat patterns are consistent with the presence of acrocentric or near telocentric centromeres in five out of 13 chromosomes (chromosomes 8, 9, 11, 12, 13). For eight more chromosomes (chromosomes 1–7, 10) we observed more diffused and fragmented patterns of repeats over a large proportion of the chromosome, suggesting the presence of polycentric centromere … it should be noted that these analyses only provide a putative centromere location" ([Hirabayashi 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/plants14010124)). "The same putative centromere type (polycentric, metacentric, acrocentric) was predicted for the different chromosomes between male and female assemblies" ([Hirabayashi 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-026-06960-7)). The method's validation rests on "159 chromosomes with experimentally-verified centromere positions from 12 plant and animal species" ([Elphinstone 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/1755-0998.14084)); no CENH3 ChIP, immunostaining or satellite characterisation exists for *U. dioica*. The only Rosales polycentric with CENH3 evidence is *Morus notabilis* ([Ma 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/hr/uhad111)).

**Rule C3.2 — MUST analyse the two centromere classes as separate strata (five "acrocentric" vs eight "diffuse/polycentric" chromosomes) and MUST test whether any genome-wide pattern is driven by centromere class before reporting it as a species-level property.**

Rationale from the comparative literature. In holocentrics the canonical centromere effect on crossovers is absent: crossovers were "abolished inside centromeric units but not in their proximity, indicating the absence of a canonical centromere effect" ([Castellani 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41477-024-01625-y)); holocentricity redefines 3D compartments and facilitates fusions ([Hofstatter 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2022.06.045)); mean chromosome size predicts recombination rate and monocentrics have nearly twice as many crossovers per chromosome as holocentrics ([Zedek 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/genetics/iyaf247)). In pea metapolycentromeres "genes, DNA methylation profiles, and most of the repeats were uniformly distributed within the centromere" ([Macas 2023](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1010633)). Hence TE density, GC, gene density and recombination proxies are expected a priori to differ by centromere class; a pooled genome-wide summary would mix two regimes. The predicted centromeres coincide with gene-density minima in nettle ([Hirabayashi 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/plants14010124), Table S8), so gene density is partly circular with the prediction and MUST NOT be used as independent support for centromere position.

**Rule C3.3 — SHOULD use the published putative-centromere coordinates (Hirabayashi 2025 Table S8; male equivalents on Figshare project 230981) rather than re-deriving them, and MUST state the source when masking centromeric intervals.**

## C4. Fusion/fission and karyotype instability

**Rule C4.1 — MUST treat *U. dioica* chromosomes 8 and 13 as a fusion/fission pair relative to *U. urens* chromosome 2, and MUST NOT use chromosome identity as a homology unit across *Urtica* species.**

Evidence. "We find that the U. dioica chromosomes 8 and 13 are syntenic with chromosome 2 in U. urens, highlighting a clear history of chromosome fusion/fission. The instability of the organization of these chromosomes within U. dioica could explain the high level of structural variation that we observe between haplotypes in chromosome 8" ([Hirabayashi 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/plants14010124)). *U. urens* drUrtUren1.1 is "339.60 megabases … scaffolded into 12 chromosomal pseudomolecules" ([Christenhusz 2024](https://doi.org/10.12688/wellcomeopenres.23187.1)). Direction of the event (fusion in *U. urens* or fission in *U. dioica*) is not established; an outgroup (ramie, *Parietaria*, *Morus*) is required before stating one.

**Rule C4.2 — MUST use synteny blocks, not chromosome numbers, for Urticaceae/Rosales comparisons, and SHOULD prefer *Boehmeria* over *Parietaria* as the within-family outgroup for block definition.**

Evidence. "Chromosomal synteny is much higher between Urtica and B. nivea than between either of them and P. judaica" despite comparable phylogenetic distance ([Hirabayashi 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/plants14010124)). In Moraceae, *M. alba* n = 14 and *M. notabilis* n = 6/7 are related by fusion/fission with high block synteny ([Xuan 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/hr/uhab030); [Xia 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gpb.2022.08.005)). Both *U. dioica* chromosomes involved (8, 13) are in the predicted acrocentric class (C3), so fusion/fission and centromere-type effects are confounded on these two chromosomes and MUST be reported together.

**Rule C4.3 — SHOULD treat the roughly two-fold genome-size difference between *U. dioica* (Cx ≈ 540–650 Mb) and *U. urens* (340 Mb) as a TE-content difference until shown otherwise, and MUST normalise TE comparisons per chromosome length rather than per genome.**

Evidence. TEs are "69.14% and 68.59% of the total sequence of H1 and H2", LTRs "47.23% and 50.51%" ([Hirabayashi 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/plants14010124)); *U. urens* genome size and chromosome number from [Christenhusz 2024](https://doi.org/10.12688/wellcomeopenres.23187.1). No TE analysis of *U. urens* has been published.

## C5. Sex

**Rule C5.1 — MUST record the sex of every individual used, MUST NOT assume an XY system when designing sex-association tests, and MUST state that male heterogamety rests on classical selfing evidence only.**

Evidence. "Evidence for male heterogamety was first obtained by Strasburger, who selfed a female plant that had formed some aberrant individual male flowers, obtaining only female progeny"; the male "is believed to be the heterogametic sex" ([Hirabayashi 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-026-06960-7)). Crossing studies show "a maternal influence on sex determination and … the possibility of gynodioecy as an intermediate stage" ([Shannon 2006](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00497-006-0041-5)); "maternal parents strongly contribute to the variation in the primary sex ratio" and reciprocal-cross progeny "resembled the sex ratios produced by their maternal parents" ([Glawe 2006](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1420-9101.2006.01215.x)); seed sex ratio ranges 0.05–0.76 among families and responds to one generation of selection ([de Jong 2005](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-2005-837540)); "true monoecy in stinging nettle is widespread: male and female flowers of different gender forms were found in 10 out of 36 genets" ([Popova 2024](https://doi.org/10.1111/1442-1984.12486)). Sister-family precedent: in cannabis and hop "no clear sex-determining genes reside on the Y. Instead, an X-linked homolog of … ACS … determines the fate of the female inflorescence" ([Carey 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-73233-7)).

Consequences. (a) A female-only reference (clone 11-4) cannot contain a male-specific region; male-specific sequence can be sought only in the male assembly (paternal haplotype per chromosome, Hirabayashi 2026 Table 2). (b) Sex-biased expression analyses do not exist and would need sexed RNA-seq; none of the 98 public RNA-seq runs is annotated for sex in the metadata retrieved. (c) Maternal effects mean that progeny sex ratios cannot be used to infer a simple Mendelian SDR genotype.

**Rule C5.2 — MUST flag any SDR claim on chromosome 8 as the data-generating group's declared next step and SHOULD NOT present an independent chromosome-8 SDR analysis as novel without citing that flag.**

Evidence. "This male assembly will be foundational to further studies aimed at characterizing the sex-determining region (SDR) in Urtica and determining the cause of the male heterogamety … a highly variable region in chromosome 8 presents several of the hallmarks of an SDR" ([Hirabayashi 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-026-06960-7)).

**Rule C6.3 — MUST label as "abstract-only" any claim here whose source was not read in full:** Rejlová 2020, Urfus 2021, Glawe 2008, Woodland 1982, Wolff 2026, and all Topic 12 comparators except where quoted from abstracts above.
